## Background

Closing the gap between benchmark performance and reliable real-world operation remains a central challenge for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) humanoid robots. These systems must handle execution errors, distribution shifts, and environmental variability that are absent from training simulations. Previous approaches have focused largely on architectural innovations rather than the practical challenges of real-world deployment.

## Core Innovation

DEED comprises three key components: (1) a data-efficient post-training pipeline with control-frequency alignment, data curation, task-relevant visual highlighting, and reduced VLA dependence; (2) a real-world study of experience-driven refinement adapted from RECAP via a text-based advantage prefix and a vision-language value function; and (3) a latent-space analysis tool for studying in- and out-of-distribution behavior. The central thesis — supported by empirical results — is that bridging the lab-to-store gap is primarily a systems integration challenge rather than an architectural one.

## Key Results

Evaluated on a supermarket chip-restocking task using a Unitree G1-Edu humanoid robot and the GR00T N1.6 foundation model, DEED shows that careful data design and targeted post-training can transform a policy that fails under naive fine-tuning into a competent real-world system using only a single GPU. The latent-space analysis tool provides interpretable diagnostics for distribution shift, enabling targeted improvements.

## Limitations

The evaluation is limited to a single retail task (chip restocking) and a specific robot platform (Unitree G1-Edu). The extent to which the DEED pipeline generalizes to other retail tasks, different humanoid platforms, or non-retail environments is not yet demonstrated. The approach relies on the GR00T N1.6 foundation model, which may not be available or optimal for all deployment scenarios.

## Industry Implications

DEED directly addresses a critical bottleneck in humanoid robot deployment: the cost and complexity of transitioning from simulated or lab-trained policies to robust real-world operation. The finding that a single GPU and careful data curation suffice to bridge the gap is highly encouraging for cost-sensitive deployment scenarios. Retail — particularly supermarket restocking and shelf management — represents a large potential market for humanoid robots, and DEED provides a practical path toward operational viability.
