## Background

Humanoid robot control demands whole-body coordination, real-time responsiveness to control signals, and robust generalization across diverse environments — making it a cornerstone challenge for generalist embodied agents. Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) have emerged as a promising solution by learning from large-scale behavioral data, yet the interplay between learning paradigm, data strategy, and model architecture for effective scaling has remained unclear.

## Core Innovation

This work presents a complete scaling recipe for BFMs built on three coordinated components:

1. **Motion Tracking Learning Paradigm**: Reformulates diverse humanoid control problems as the reproduction of integrated whole-body behaviors in the global coordinate frame, unifying walking, manipulation, and balance under a single training objective.
2. **Strategic Data Synergy**: Demonstrates that on-policy rollout quantity and reference motion diversity act synergistically — scaling both together yields disproportionate gains over scaling either alone.
3. **Humanoid Transformer Architecture**: An expressive and scalable Transformer design that enables the natural emergence of structured behavioral representations without explicit modular engineering.

## Results

Extensive experiments in simulation and real-world deployment show:

- **Over 10% reduction** in Mean Per-Keypoint Position Error (MPKPE) in local mode
- **82% reduction** in MPKPE in global mode — a decisive margin over existing methods
- Significant improvements in control fidelity and task generalization
- Structured, interpretable behavioral representations that emerge naturally from the architecture

## Limitations

The paper does not extensively characterize performance under extreme conditions such as complex terrain or high-dynamic interaction. Computational overhead of the Humanoid Transformer relative to lightweight alternatives is not compared. Real-world deployment scope and task diversity remain limited.

## Industry Implications

This work represents **a significant milestone toward general-purpose foundation models for humanoid robots**. With Tesla Optimus, Figure 01, and other platforms accelerating toward production, a scalable BFM with open architecture can directly power next-generation humanoid products. The reproducibility of the scaling recipe and the potential for open-sourcing the Humanoid Transformer make this a potential industry benchmark.
