Handroid addresses a fundamental design tension in robotics: dexterous hands and humanoid robots have traditionally been developed as separate embodiments, each optimized for different scales and tasks. The authors propose a single 27-DoF electromechanical platform that physically reconfigures between a dexterous anthropomorphic hand and a 0.33m desktop humanoid weighing just 2.05 kg.

**Core innovation.** The key insight is that the same articulated modules can be reused: in hand mode, 20 DoFs form an anthropomorphic hand matching human kinematic structure; in humanoid mode, the modules reconfigure into a body with head, arms, and legs, including a 12-DoF lower limb structure for locomotion and whole-body motion. A unified control and learning framework supports both embodiments, enabling hand teleoperation, grasping, in-hand manipulation, humanoid locomotion, gait generation, and interactive motion authoring.

**Experimental results.** The team validated the platform across multiple real-world benchmarks: dexterous grasping on diverse objects, reinforcement-learning-based locomotion with gait emergence, keyframe-based motion deployment, and a long-horizon task that chains embodiment reconfiguration, locomotion, docking, and dexterous pick-and-place. No simulation-only results — all experiments involve real hardware.

**Limitations.** At 0.33 m height, Handroid is a desktop-scale platform; it cannot match the payload or reach of full-size humanoids. The 2.05 kg weight limit restricts the types of objects it can manipulate. Long-horizon tasks requiring multiple reconfigurations add mechanical wear and calibration drift over time.

**Industry implications.** Handroid's reconfigurable design could accelerate research in morphology-reconfigurable robotics and cross-embodiment learning at a fraction of the cost of multiple specialized platforms. As humanoid robotics investment surges, compact platforms like Handroid provide an accessible testbed for whole-body manipulation and locomotion algorithms without requiring expensive full-scale hardware.
