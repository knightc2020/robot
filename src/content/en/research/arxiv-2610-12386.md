*The robot learning field has a default answer to weak policies: collect more data and train a bigger model. That answer is expensive and slow, and ARC is a reminder that the same foundation models can improve dramatically if they are taught to reason about their actions in the right way.*

### Background

Improving robot foundation models today usually means scaling: larger models, more robot demonstrations, and costly training runs. This is effective but costly, and it leaves an obvious question open — how much of the gap between current models and better ones is capacity, and how much is the supervision they are given? ARC takes the position that there exists an effective and efficient complementary approach: the right reasoning recipe can substantially improve the zero-shot task performance of existing state-of-the-art robot foundation models, without new robot data or foundation-scale training.

### Core Innovation

ARC consists of three ingredients. First, it specifies what a useful reasoning trace looks like: it should be grounded in the robot's next action and explain that action's causal structure — why the action is appropriate and what effect it should produce. This grounding distinguishes a trace that helps control from generic commentary. Second, it provides a scalable automatic labeling pipeline that generates these traces from existing demonstrations, which means ARC-Trace-DROID can be constructed from DROID without collecting any new robot data. Third, it gives a strategy for adapting pretrained robot foundation models to use the traces for control, with fine-tuning and inference tailored to each model's architecture and capabilities. The abstract notes this works for state-of-the-art VLAs such as pi_0.5 and world-action models such as Cosmos3-Nano-Policy.

### Results

- ARC establishes a new state of the art on RoboLab-120 and MolmoSpaces.
- It yields gains of up to 50 percentage points on RoboLab-Reasoning-50.
- On real robots, ARC improves pi_0.5's task success by 82.2 percentage points.
- The gains are obtained without additional robot demonstrations or foundation-scale training.
- ARC-Trace-DROID is generated automatically from DROID.

### Limitations

The abstract presents headline gains but not the scope behind them: it does not report the number of real-robot tasks, the variance across runs, or how the 82.2-point improvement on pi_0.5 distributes across tasks, so it is hard to tell whether the effect is uniform or driven by a subset. The claim that gains are "unprecedented" is asserted rather than benchmarked against a sweep of alternative supervision recipes. Because the traces are auto-labeled from existing demonstrations, the method inherits the distribution and biases of those demonstrations, and the abstract does not quantify how much added inference cost or latency the reasoning traces introduce at control time — a central concern when the selling point is efficiency. The adaptation strategy is described per-architecture but not analyzed for how brittle it is across model families.

### Industry Implications

If a reasoning recipe can move zero-shot performance by tens of points without new data, then the bottleneck for many teams is not data volume but supervision design, and that is a much cheaper thing to change. The auto-labeling pipeline is the most directly reusable asset: it turns an existing demonstration corpus into trace supervision, which means the investment in data already made keeps paying. For teams building on commercial VLA and world-action backbones, the message is that the model is not the only lever — how it is taught to reason about its next action may be worth more than the next round of scaling. The open question for adopters is the inference budget: reasoning traces are only a bargain if their control-time cost stays small, which the paper does not yet quantify.
