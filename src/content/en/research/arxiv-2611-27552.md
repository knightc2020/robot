Whole-body trackers for humanoids can already reproduce fairly complex motion, provided somebody hands them an entire reference trajectory. Real task instructions rarely arrive in that form. An operator cares where the robot ends up, what posture it holds there, and when it should be there. How it travels the intervening distance, and at what pace, is left to a separate planner. Between knowing how to track and being able to finish a task sits a gap that has to be filled by hand.

S-BFM starts from human motor coordination: given a start and an endpoint, people do not plan frame by frame. A low-level controller organises the intervening motion and its timing by itself. The paper turns that capacity into a behavioral foundation model whose only inputs are a target posture, a target pose and an arrival time.

### Core innovation

Three components are chained together. Spatial grounding first adds world-frame position and heading to a forward-backward behavior representation through spatial reconstruction. A forward-backward representation captures the ordering and direction of a motion well but is inherently blind to where in the world it happens; this step restores the missing metric information.

Time-conditioned distillation comes next. A teacher that depends on dense reference trajectories is imitated in closed loop, so the student learns not just the shape of the motion but how long that distance should take. Pace becomes a learned quantity rather than an interpolation bolted on at inference.

Generative fine-tuning then uses a distilled motion generator to widen the range of distances and arrival times seen during training. This step decides whether the model keeps a natural gait when asked to cover ground well beyond its training distribution.

### Results

In simulation, under both fixed and mixed horizons, S-BFM lowers global joint-position error by more than 32% against the best baseline. Fixed-goal reach rises from 43.5% to 74.6% while terminal position error falls from 1.67 m to 0.26 m. The pair matters together: higher reach with lower terminal error means the model is not simply charging past the goal and correcting afterwards.

The authors further deploy the system on a full-size AGIBOT A3 humanoid, showing that sparse spatiotemporal goals suffice to drive natural, well-timed whole-body motion.

### Limitations

No quantitative hardware numbers appear in the abstract. Running on an A3 is a meaningful credibility signal, but with no success rate, tracking error or timing deviation reported, the hardware generalisation boundary stays undefined.

Training data scale and motion coverage are also unstated, and they determine how the model behaves on unseen goal combinations. Inference rate, parameter count and onboard compute requirements are absent as well, even though deployability of a whole-body controller depends heavily on exactly those figures. The abstract describes successes only; how the model degrades when a goal is unreachable or conflicts with the current posture is not analysed.

### Industry implications

The contribution is a shift in where the interface sits. Humanoid teams used to write a planner that emits reference trajectories and train a tracker to follow them, then tune the seam between the two. S-BFM folds the how-to-get-there into the controller so the planner emits only sparse waypoints and times. A thinner interface means less integration engineering.

That sparse interface matches how real deployments phrase work. Inspection points, station handovers and pick-and-place positions are naturally expressed as a location plus a posture plus a deadline. A controller that organises the transition and its timing on its own removes a layer of task-orchestration complexity.

One caveat deserves weight. Simulation numbers sit some distance from a full-size body with actuator delay and contact uncertainty. The freedom granted by sparse goals is both the strength and the risk: the model holds more authority over the path taken, and whether a transition without explicit geometric constraints stays safe around obstacles or changing terrain is not answered here.
