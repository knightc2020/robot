## Background

Contact-rich robot manipulation requires physical interaction cues that cameras cannot capture, making tactile sensing essential for robust control. However, scaling visuo-tactile robot learning is difficult because real tactile interaction data is expensive to collect, hardware-dependent, and limited in task and scene diversity. No prior work has demonstrated a world model capable of predicting both visual observations and tactile feedback from actions.

## Core Innovation

ViTacWorld introduces an action-conditioned visuo-tactile world model that predicts temporally aligned visual observations and tactile feedback given robot actions. The key insight is that tactile signals are directly grounded in physical contact and exhibit a smaller simulation-to-real gap than purely visual observations, making sim-to-real transfer more feasible for tactile data. The model is first pretrained with large-scale real and simulated visuo-tactile trajectories, then finetuned with real-world policy rollouts. To the best of the authors' knowledge, this is the first framework that uses a world model for robot visuo-tactile-action trajectory generation and policy evaluation.

## Key Results

ViTacWorld serves two roles: synthesizing rollouts to improve downstream tactile policies, and evaluating policies by predicting action-conditioned visuo-tactile outcomes under controlled action sequences. Experiments on contact-rich manipulation tasks show that ViTacWorld generates physically meaningful rollouts, improves policy performance through scalable data augmentation, and enables action-conditioned policy evaluation. The framework leverages both public real tactile datasets and a constructed simulation environment for data scaling.

## Limitations

The quality of generated tactile feedback depends on the fidelity of the tactile simulator used during pretraining. The framework has been demonstrated on contact-rich manipulation but its applicability to high-speed dynamic tasks (e.g., impact or sliding) remains unvalidated. Real-world finetuning still requires some policy rollouts, though far fewer than training from scratch.

## Industry Implications

Tactile sensing is a critical bottleneck for deploying robots in tasks that require delicate contact — assembly, surgical assistance, food handling, and material sorting. ViTacWorld's ability to generate synthetic visuo-tactile trajectories at scale could dramatically reduce the data collection burden for tactile robotics. Its dual role as both a data augmentation tool and a policy evaluator makes it a practical asset for any robotics lab working on contact-rich manipulation, potentially accelerating the adoption of tactile sensing in industrial applications.
