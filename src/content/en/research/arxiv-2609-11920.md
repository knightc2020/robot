Reliable relative localization is a precondition for tightly coordinated heterogeneous air-ground teams. Conventional solutions rely on frame-based cameras and fiducial markers, which are sensitive to motion blur and lighting changes and add payload and compute burden.

### Core Innovation

The paper introduces **EVPeriscope**, an event-based perception system in which a ground robot carries an **upward-facing event camera** and exploits the **high-frequency visual signature of a quadrotor's propellers** to detect, localize, and control the drone. Because event cameras record only brightness changes, they are naturally robust to motion blur and lighting variation and are well suited to capturing fast-spinning propellers. The quadrotor can then serve as **extended perception** for the ground robot when the ground robot's own sensors degrade or are occluded — a marsupial ground-aerial system in which team coordination itself becomes a source of perception redundancy.

### Results

- Field experiments in **winds up to roughly 15 mph**, in both **daylight and at night**.
- Localization and **closed-loop navigation through dense foliage** where the ground robot's sensors are occluded.
- Quadrotor control at **200 Hz**, **entirely with onboard sensing and computation** rather than external positioning infrastructure.
- Experiment videos are available on the project page: https://ongdexter.github.io/evperiscope

### Limitations

The abstract is qualitative: it reports no localization accuracy, effective range, or number of trials, and offers no quantitative comparison against frame-based or fiducial-marker baselines, so the claimed advantage cannot be measured from the summary. Performance still depends on lighting and on the propellers remaining visible in the field of view; the demonstrations involve a single quadrotor and a moderate 15 mph wind, leaving more extreme conditions and multi-drone formations unexplored. The system relies on a custom event camera and onboard compute, and the abstract discloses neither code nor a hardware bill of materials.

### Industry Implications

For inspection, search and rescue, and subterranean or under-canopy settings, the appeal is **trading hardware for perception**: ground platforms already need dependable air-ground relative localization, and event cameras are low-power and compact enough for robots already near their payload limits. The approach also gives the "drone as an external sensor" idea a concrete localization mechanism — when the host platform's sensors are wet, occluded, or failed, the heterogeneous team's coordination itself becomes the redundant source of state.
