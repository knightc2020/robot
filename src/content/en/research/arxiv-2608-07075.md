## Background

Delicate manipulation often involves brief, subtle collisions between a grasped object and its surroundings. Humans localize these contacts effortlessly thanks to dense tactile sensitivity; robots generally cannot. Without knowing where and when a grasped object touches the environment, motion planning proceeds blindly, and manipulation becomes clumsy or fails outright. Most robotic systems lack the sensing resolution needed to capture transient extrinsic contacts — contacts that are not at the robot's own joints but between the payload and the world — which is exactly the information needed to plan around them.

## Core Innovation

TECDAR (transient extrinsic contact detection and ranging) is built on a strikingly compact sensing element: a single 2.5 x 3 mm 6D inertial measurement unit (IMU) embedded in each gripper tip. The IMU measures sub-millisecond tip deformations at a 7 kHz sampling rate while producing a data stream of only 84 KB/s — high bandwidth, tiny data footprint. Tactile data are fused with robot pose in an extended Kalman filter, producing fast, precise localization of extrinsic contact. The design deliberately avoids bulky force-torque arrays or vision systems, keeping the sensing local, cheap, and fast.

## Results

In real experiments, the system reaches an average localization accuracy of approximately 7 mm for both line-contact and point-contact tasks, and detects and localizes contact within 180 ms. Because localization is near-instantaneous, the robot can rectify its trajectory on a millisecond scale during a task rather than after a failure. The authors demonstrate precise tool manipulation and improved perception of complex environments driven purely by tactile exploration and mapping, showing that the high-bandwidth, low-data design is sufficient for closed-loop contact reasoning.

## Limitations

The evaluation focuses on localization accuracy and trajectory-rectification capability in controlled settings; long-horizon, unstructured tasks are not yet demonstrated. The 7 mm accuracy, while strong, may be insufficient for sub-millimeter precision assembly in some use cases. The approach is demonstrated on gripper tips with an integrated IMU, so applying it to other end-effector geometries requires redesign of the sensing tip.

## Industry Implications

Extrinsic contact sensing is one of the missing pieces in reliable autonomous manipulation: assembly cells, surgical assistants, and mobile manipulators all operate in environments where the payload will occasionally touch something unexpected. TECDAR's combination of 7 kHz bandwidth, 84 KB/s data rate, and millimeter-level localization in under 200 ms is directly relevant to precision assembly, surgical assistance, and autonomous exploration in touch-dominant environments. The low data footprint also makes it attractive for embedded and untethered robots where compute and power budgets are tight.
