## Background

Safe navigation within the spinal subarachnoid space is severely constrained by its narrow, compliant, and delicate anatomy. Conventional catheters and continuum robots rely on proximal pushing, generating friction and shear along the tissue-device interface that limits distal controllability and increases neural injury risk. Despite advances in minimally invasive spine techniques, no platform has demonstrated the ability to safely advance within this confined space while simultaneously providing real-time visualization.

## Core Innovation

This work introduces a 2 mm diameter eversion-growing robotic platform that enables low-friction extension and steering within the human spinal subarachnoid space. The key innovation is pressure-driven tip eversion, which localizes motion to the distal tip while minimizing translational sliding of the deployed body. The robot integrates a miniature endoscope for real-time intrathecal visualization. The team validated the system through computational modeling, phantom experiments, and intact human cadaver studies — a multimodality approach that strengthens the translational evidence.

## Key Results

Phantom experiments demonstrated 65.2% reduction in mean interaction force and 48.0% reduction in peak interaction force compared to matched push-based insertion. Physics-based modeling showed that eversion growth redistributes tissue loading, reducing local stress concentrations and interfacial shear. In an intact human cadaver, the system achieved 150 mm of controlled intrathecal extension with concurrent fluoroscopic and endoscopic visualization from a standard lumbar entry point. Postprocedural laminectomy and durotomy revealed no observable macroscopic disruption of the dura mater or surrounding neural structures.

## Limitations

The principal limitation is that further validation in larger anatomical cohorts and under physiological conditions (CSF pulsation, patient positioning) is required before clinical translation. Results are from a single cadaver specimen, and long-term tissue effects and biocompatibility remain unassessed.

## Industry Implications

This technology opens a new minimally invasive pathway for intrathecal interventions including drug delivery, electrode implantation, and tumor biopsy. For the large patient population suffering from spinal disorders, chronic pain, and neurological conditions, the ability to safely access the spinal subarachnoid space has enormous clinical potential. The eversion-growing design principle may also extend to interventions in other luminal organs.
